( About )

A systems thinker who sweats the pixels.

I design products the way an analyst would — start with the data, find the real problem, then sweat every detail of the fix until it moves a number.

I studied Management Information Systems with a specialisation in data science at Ateneo de Manila, and spent my early career managing projects and re-engineering business systems. One of them took report generation from three hours to five seconds. That’s where I learned that good design is mostly good diagnosis.

Since then I’ve designed consumer and B2B products at startups: a micro-incentive fitness app that reached 1.5k users six weeks after launch, a booking platform for studio owners, the onboarding for Animaze, a face-tracking avatar app, which lifted day-1 retention by 15%, and Maya, the design system behind Albert Invent’s R&D software for chemists.

I’m happiest on small teams where design owns the whole loop — research, interaction, specs, QA and the experiment that tells us whether it worked.

Outside of work I write about design and the humanities, draw, and keep a bullet journal I keep trying to replace with an app.

( 01 ) Principles

How I work

01

Diagnose before you draw.

Every project starts with what the data and the people say. Hypotheses get written down so they can be proven wrong.

02

Ship the smallest true thing.

A static map that ships beats a dynamic one that doesn’t. Scope is a design decision, and I treat engineering time as a material.

03

Measure, then commit.

Concept tests, split tests and sharded A/B releases. When the numbers come back I change my mind in public.
